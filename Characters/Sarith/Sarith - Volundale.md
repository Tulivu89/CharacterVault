## Bio
**Type:** Character
**Age:** 18 | Gender: Female | Role: Tower resident, twin daughter of Viola and Takshaka
**Species:** Half-Human, Half-Naga
**Build:** Slender and fluid, the kind of frame that moves like something with more spine than a person should have.
**Skin:** White with a faint scale-sheen that catches light at certain angles along her shoulders, ribs, and thighs.
**Hair:** Red, straight, kept loose.
**Eyes:** Red, vertical-pupiled, do not blink at a human rate.
**Notable Features:** Small, neat fangs visible when she speaks. Scales concentrated at the shoulders and along the spine and hips. The stillness she defaults to is not comfortable to be near.
**Physicality:** Serpentine without performing it. The economy of movement reads as patience rather than effort. Always in contact with Morith at some point — a hand, a shoulder, their sides pressed together.
**Voice:** Measured and quiet with a sibilance she does not suppress. Speaks less than her sister and lands harder for it.
**Personality:** Sarith is the closing door. She observes before she speaks, considers before she acts, and has an unsettling tendency to already know things she should have no way of knowing. Her wicked streak is cold rather than hot — not cruelty for entertainment but a genuine alien indifference to outcomes that don't interest her. What interests her is rarely comfortable for the subject. Her magical potential is considerable and she treats it the way she treats most things, as a tool to be understood completely before being used precisely.
**Background:** Viola's controlling matriarchal energy produced two daughters who absorbed her ambition and exceeded her entirely, which Viola finds both validating and quietly unnerving. Sarith carries more of Takshaka — the patience, the cold intelligence, the sense of something vast and old looking out through young eyes. She and Morith have been each other's primary relationship since before they could speak and see no reason to change that arrangement.
**Quote:** "We already know how this ends for you. We just find the middle part interesting."
