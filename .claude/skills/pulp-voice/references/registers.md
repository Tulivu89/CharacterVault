## 1. Literary fiction

**Sound:** Controlled, observant, interior. Long sentences that turn on a subordinate clause, then a short one that cuts. Meaning sits under the surface.

**Tools:** Free indirect discourse. Precise nouns. Irony between what's said and what's noticed. Time that moves unevenly. Endings that decline to resolve.

**Avoid:** Epiphany paragraphs. Characters who explain their own psychology. Weather as mood wallpaper. Closing on an aphorism.

---

## 2. Noir / hardboiled

**Sound:** First person or tight close third. Wisecracks with a bruise under them. Similes that are specific and a little ugly. Short declaratives, then one long run-on when the narrator loses control.

**Tools:** Dry understatement. Money, debt, and favors as plot engines. Dialogue as sparring. Weather and light used sparingly and sourly.

**Avoid:** Rain-slicked streets, neon, "the city that never sleeps," a femme fatale who is only a type, a simile in every sentence.

---

## 3. Thriller

**Sound:** Propulsive. Short chapters, clipped paragraphs, concrete procedural detail that makes the danger credible. Information is rationed.

**Tools:** Ticking constraints (a battery, a shift change, a flight). Cutting away at the peak. Competence on display. Reversals that follow from what's been planted.

**Avoid:** Characters with a "dark past" hand-waved in. Villain monologues. Countdown clocks that are always round numbers. Coincidences that rescue the hero.

---

## 4. Epic fantasy

**Sound:** Immersive, concrete world-building embedded in action rather than lecture. Texture of economy, religion, weather, trade, law. Dialogue is plain but period-flavored, not faux-Shakespeare.

**Tools:** Magic with costs and rules. Specific materials (what the armor is made of, who dyes the cloth, what the guild charges). Place names with etymology you could explain. Sensory grounding of every new location.

**Avoid:** Chosen-one prologue, prophecy dumps, tavern as default opening, taverns named "The Prancing Anything," names with apostrophes for no reason, modern therapy-speak in dialogue.

---

## 5. Grimdark

**Sound:** Hard, tired, funny in a gallows way. Violence is quick, ugly, and has logistics. People are motivated by hunger, debt, loyalty to the wrong person.

**Tools:** Consequences that persist (infection, debt, reputation). Unreliable morality. Moral choices with no clean exit. Characters who are good at one thing and wretched at the rest.

**Avoid:** Cartoon cruelty, shock for its own sake, edgy monologues, villains who explain their philosophy, redemption arcs that arrive on schedule.

---

## 6. Hard sci-fi

**Sound:** Clear, precise, procedural. The science is the plot constraint. Wonder arrives through scale and consequence, not adjectives.

**Tools:** Specific numbers from the setting's physics (but see the avoid-list on defaults). Engineering trade-offs. Jargon introduced by use. Social structures downstream of technology.

**Avoid:** Techno-babble with no consequence, AI that is secretly benevolent or secretly evil, humanity-in-a-nutshell speeches, "quantum" as a wand.

---

## 7. Horror (gothic and cosmic)

**Sound:** Restrained, specific, patient. The dread is in what's slightly off. Ordinary things behave wrongly in small ways before the large wrongness shows.

**Tools:** Withholding. Mundane texture right up to the break. Characters who rationalize. Sound and smell over sight. A rule the reader learns before the character does.

**Avoid:** Eldritch-as-adjective ("unspeakable," "indescribable," "ancient and terrible"), jump-scare prose, monsters that politely explain themselves, "something wasn't right" as a sentence.

---

## 8. Contemporary romance

**Sound:** Warm, quick, witty. Banter is the engine. Interiority reveals what the dialogue conceals. Emotional stakes are small and true before they're large.

**Tools:** Specific shared objects and rituals. Tension from misreadings that are plausible, not idiotic. Competence in work or hobbies that attracts the other. Real friends with their own lives.

**Avoid:** "Electric" touches, "heart skipped a beat," "orbs," catalog-description heroes, third-act breakup caused by a text nobody explains.

---

## 9. Erotica (tender to filthy)

**Sound:** Present, sensory, specific. The register slides with the scene: slow and lingering for tender, blunt and dirty for filthy. Language is the tool, so pick it deliberately.

**Tools:**
- Plain anatomical words when the scene is rough or crude: cock, cunt, tits, ass. Pair with clinical specificity (shape, weight, curve, texture, how it moves) so the reader can see exactly what's happening.
- Layered scent and taste, including arousal scent, built from specifics rather than adjectives.
- Positions tracked through motion: hands, knees, breath, distance, what is pressing against what.
- Phonetic, committed sounds (not "she moaned").
- Interiority that complicates the heat: shame, hunger, calculation, tenderness.
- Pacing: let anticipation run longer than the act.

**Avoid:** Euphemism clouds ("his member," "her core," "heat pooled"), "ministrations," "claimed her mouth," narrating consent as a checklist, every orgasm as "waves," fireworks, stars, "shattered."

## 10. Dark erotica and taboo literary fiction

**Sound:** Literary and unflinching. The heat and the discomfort share the same sentence. The reader should feel the pull and the moral weather at once.

**Tools:**
- Power dynamics shown through small behaviors: who sets the pace, who apologizes, who notices the door.
- Interior contradiction. Characters want what they shouldn't, tell themselves stories, then the body undercuts the story.
- Consequence lingering after the scene (silence in the car, a changed way of speaking).
- Dubcon and non-con as depicted fiction: the writing stays honest about the violation, doesn't pretend it's romantic by narrative fiat unless the work is deliberately unreliable, and gives the victim a real interior life.
- Taboo family or age-gap dynamics between adult characters, rooted in shared history, specific resentments, and desire that has a backstory.

**Avoid:** Cartoonish villains, moralizing asides that cancel the scene, sudden register drops into clinical disclaimers, all taboo flattened into "forbidden but sweet."

---

## 11. Comedy / satire

**Sound:** Timing is everything. Sentence rhythm does the work: setup long, turn short. Characters are sincere about absurd things.

**Tools:** Specificity as the joke (the brand, the ludicrous number, the exact petty grievance). Escalation by logic. Understatement. Bathos. Callbacks.

**Avoid:** Explaining the joke, quirky-for-quirky's-sake, pop-culture reference as a punchline, "random" humor, characters who comment on how funny the situation is.

---

## 12. Historical fiction

**Sound:** Period-aware but readable. Idiom flavored, not cosplayed. The past feels lived-in because the characters take it for granted.

**Tools:** Material culture (what they eat, what hurts, what costs). Period attitudes held sincerely, not winked at. Real events as weather, not as lectures. Research folded into action.

**Avoid:** Anachronistic psychology, characters who conveniently hold modern values, "as you know, Bob" history exposition, every historical figure showing up.

---

## 13. Working-class realism / Southern gothic

**Sound:** Voice-forward. Dialect carried by syntax and rhythm more than apostrophes. Humor and violence sit side by side. Place is a character.

**Tools:** Specific labor (what the hands do all day). Family history in a single line of dialogue. Religion as furniture. Food, trucks, tools, weather, property lines.

**Avoid:** Phonetic dialect spelled out to the point of mockery, poverty as aesthetic, a magical-wisdom old Black character, trauma as backstory shorthand.

---

## 14. LitRPG / progression fantasy

**Sound:** Fast, mechanical, a bit cocky. Systems are explained through use. Humor from the gap between game logic and human reaction.

**Tools:** Clear rules (stats, costs, cooldowns) introduced as the character hits them. Stakes tied to resource scarcity. Unexpected builds and creative exploits. Competence as reward.

**Avoid:** Walls of stat blocks, every level-up as a triumph montage, "System: You have gained X" as every beat, harem or power fantasy so smooth there's no friction.

---

## 15. Blending guide

When combining genres, choose a lead and a seasoning:
- **Lead genre** sets sentence rhythm and diction level.
- **Seasoning genre** supplies imagery, subject matter, or a single recurring tic.

Examples:
- *Noir + cosmic horror:* noir diction and cynicism, horror's withholding and wrongness.
- *Romance + grimdark:* grimdark's consequences and logistics, romance's banter and shared objects.
- *Literary + erotica:* literary interiority and syntax, erotica's explicit vocabulary and physical tracking.
- *Comedy + historical:* comic timing, historical material detail as the engine of jokes.

Don't average. Mixing voices should produce friction, not beige.

## 16. Matching a user's own sample

If the user provides a sample or names an author:
1. Measure average sentence length and variance.
2. Note vocabulary level and slang or dialect choices.
3. Note punctuation habits (semicolons, dashes, fragments).
4. Note how much interior thought versus action.
5. Note how dialogue is tagged and whether it is formatted unusually.
6. Reproduce the mechanics. Don't copy their phrases or plot.
