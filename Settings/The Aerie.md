# The Aerie
> Marked fae climb a smoking mountain to be chosen by dragons. There are more cadets than dragons, and the way down is no easier than the way up.

*Untagged lines are canon. **[draft]** marks proposals waiting on approval.*

## Premise
A dragon rider cadet school in the [[Aedling Kingdom]] of [[Volungraiv]], inspired by Fourth Wing but its own setting. Every cadet is an adult (18–19). The protagonist is left open so the setting works for creative writing and AI roleplay. The story arcs through the stages of rider school, from the climb to bonding to signature powers and beyond.

The core cast is female fae cadets, each with her own personality, desires and hang-ups, built to clash with the protagonist and with each other. Their dragons are full characters too, with ties to other dragons. Everything is designed as a source of conflict.

## Tone
Heavy Metal and Brütal Legend. Grounded and grim, deep magics, album-art aesthetic, dark humor. Death is common and leaves marks on the survivors.

## Core rules
- **Titan blood.** The titans were primal, titanic creatures of a past age. Some fae served them as priestesses and bore their offspring. Fae descended from those lines carry titan blood.
- **Marks.** Now and then a bloodline produces a fae who can channel titanic magic. Skin markings reveal it in adulthood.
  - Every mark comes from bloodline. None of them come from a cadet's past, culture or family custom.
  - A mark can take almost any design: script, runes, vines, patterns and more. It can be metallic, it can glow, and so on.
  - A mark doesn't tie its bearer to any particular dragon.
  - Only fae are marked, as far as anyone currently knows.
- **Selection.** The mark comes before the school and is what selects a cadet for the Aerie. Selection is crown law: every marked fae is claimed for the Aerie. Some regions hide their marked daughters, which is treason.
- **Dragons.** Dragons also descend from titans, with no mortal blood crossed in. That shared descent is what makes a bond possible.
- **The bond.** A mark doesn't guarantee a bond, so cadets compete for dragons. The bond is what lets a rider channel what the mark promised. The signature power surfaces after bonding, shaped by the mark's titan line and by the dragon.
  - **When bonds happen.** The Kindling is the agreed time for bonding, but a dragon can bond a cadet whenever it likes. There's a saying: *Where does a ten-ton dragon sit? Wherever he wants.*
  - **Cadets who never bond.** A marked cadet who survives without bonding can repeat the year, or drop out for mage school.
- **Bloodlines.** Houses with strong bloodlines produce riders more often.
  - Mortal bloodlines are somewhat mixed, so it isn't always clear which titan line will manifest.
  - The high houses take extra pride in their ties to specific titan lines, but the line a house claims is never guaranteed to show up.
  - Nobody knows whether a dragon's brood has to match its rider's titan line, or whether a match only strengthens the pull.
  - **[draft]** A dragon's pull toward a rider can hint at which line she really carries. Sometimes it isn't the one her house claims.

## Bloodline mating
- Houses place great value on marked females bearing offspring, because every marked child raises the odds of the house producing more.
- If it were allowed, many houses wouldn't send their daughters to the Aerie at all. They'd pair them off for the house's own benefit. Crown law takes the daughters anyway, and the pressure to pair follows them up the mountain.
- Any other candidate who looks desirable quickly gets considered as a potential pair. Even a lowborn, if powerful enough, may be approached to wed, or at least to mate.
- Neither the crown nor the order has any claim on a rider's children, or any say in who she pairs with. The crown only wields influence over such matters, and the order cares only about the dragons.
- Every cadet has her own feelings about this, and few of them are wallflowers.
- **[draft]** The mark law sets houses against the crown, which feeds Crown ↔ Confederation. Pairing also gives the Blood axis a body: who gets courted, who gets passed over, and who gets bought.

## The wider world
- **The Aedling Kingdom** is large and diverse, and struggling with what that entails.
- The fae kin roughly represent regions. Written so far: Ashkin, Lunarkin, Woedkin, Solarkin. Visual builds live in *Elven Dragon Riders — Kin Designs (rev 3)*. The markings in those builds are for image generation only and don't set a cadet's canon mark.
- **Threats:**
  - Rival kingdoms, which have riders of their own or witch queens carrying more titan blood.
  - Titan remnants: diminutive and feral beside the titans of old, but still dangerous.
  - Internal conflict.
- **The civil war.** There's no civil war now, but there was one two generations ago, when some Ashkin and Woedkin factions rebelled.
- **Mage school.** Where marked cadets who never bond can go instead.
- Beastkin exist in the setting but aren't necessarily part of this scenario.
- **Kin tilts.** Each kin leans a default way on the axes below. Every kin has at least one cadet who breaks from it.
  - **Solarkin**: the heartland and the court. They lean Crown. Other kin call them *gilts*.
  - **Lunarkin**: the temple-holds and the old priestess lines. They lean Divine. Other kin call them *chalks*.
  - **Ashkin**: the forge-holds and mines. They lean Exploit. Other kin call them *cinders*.
  - **Woedkin**: the forest marches. They lean Conserve and Confederation. Other kin call them *stumps*.

## The Aerie
- The school sits at the peak of a semi-dormant volcano. Dragons perch on the peak, but most of the available dragons nest in the huge caverns inside the mountain.
- The buildings are a temple made by forgotten giants. Nobody knows where it came from, and it stays that way. It suits dragons and fae alike.
- **The first test is scaling the mountain.** Going back down is always its own challenge.

**Atmosphere**
The climb has no road. Cadets go up the rock face with their hands, and with rope if they brought it. The worst ledges are named for the cadets who fell from them, and the upperclassmen rename them every year. At the top, the temple's steps come up to a fae's hip. The halls are tall enough for a dragon to walk through with its head up. Some do, and the cadets press flat against the walls until they pass. Heat comes up through vents in the floor, and the flagstones are warm at dawn even under snow. There's sulphur in the bread.

## Who runs it
- **The Primaris Order.** A monastic order of fae who see themselves as keepers of the old titan cults, the cults the bloodlines began in. They would return the world to the age of titans, but they defer to whatever the dragons, the titans' descendants, desire. They maintain the spaces dragons and fae share, and anything else the dragons ask for.
- **The instructors.** Cadets are trained by crown military.
- **The agreement.** The Aerie runs on a negotiated agreement between the Primaris Order, the crown and the dragons.

## Life and death
- Death is common. It comes from tests with no guardrails, from cadet sparring taken all the way, and from dragons simply being dragons.
- **[draft]** The kitchens cook for the full intake all through first year and never adjust the count. Nobody goes hungry after the first month.

## Arc
1. **The Mark** (before the story). The mark surfaces and the cadet is sent to the Aerie.
2. **The Climb.** Scaling the mountain. It tests nerve and shows who helps whom.
3. **Culling season.** The first weeks: unguarded tests and sparring to the end. Alliances form and kin loyalty comes under pressure.
4. **The Kindling.** The agreed time when dragons choose, down in the nesting caverns. Faith comes to the surface: is the choice holy, or just animal? Cadets who don't bond can repeat the year or leave for mage school.
5. **The Waking.** Signature powers surface after bonding. Blood politics come to the surface: whose house had priestesses, and whose power came in strong. Pairing offers from the houses pour in for whoever comes in strong.
6. **Wing games.** Cadets against cadets, kin against kin. Crown against Confederation.
7. **First blood.** A real deployment against one of the threats, or against a wild dragon that keeps mortal slaves. Conserve against Exploit stops being an argument: do you burn the valley to win?

## Conflict axes
*These apply to cadets, not dragons.*
- **Magic: Conserve ↔ Exploit.** Some want a sustainable approach to magic. Others believe the state can't afford to fall behind its rivals. For some, if their side falls before the world does, nothing else matters.
- **Power: Crown ↔ Confederation.** Centralized power for effective governance, against decentralized power that lets diverse groups follow their own directives beyond shared ideals.
- **Faith: Divine ↔ Forces.** The titans as divine beings who embody ideals to follow and intelligences to appease, against the titans as natural forces to be respected and used only when they benefit you.
- **Blood: House ↔ Merit.** Strong houses produce more riders. Plain racism runs alongside that and splits fae against each other, both within a kin and between kin. Bloodline mating is where this axis gets personal.
- Each cadet holds open stances on most axes and wavers on one. The wavering axis is her arc.

## Dragons
- Dragons are mostly beyond mortal matters, and their reasons are often their own. The cadet axes don't apply to them.
- **Body plan.** Mostly true western dragons: four limbs and wings.
- **Speech.** Telepathic.
  - A dragon can speak to anyone it chooses. In most cases it chooses only its own rider, or its mate's rider.
  - Dragons speak to each other freely. Among themselves they also have a tongue of their own, which nobody else can follow.
- **Breath.** Every dragon breathes some form of fire, though the color and how focused it is can vary.
  - **Wytchfire**: colorful and napalm-like, it clings.
  - **Near-plasma**: crackling beams.
  - **Pyroclastic ash**: spewing clouds of it.
  - **Gas bombs**: burning balls of gas that explode on impact.
  - Most dragons can also breathe a plain stream of flame.
- **Why dragons bond.** After the primal age ended, an endless tide of mortals began raiding every inch of land, breaking or stealing eggs. Bonded dragons don't have to worry about that. Bonding gives dragons a future with dragons in it, and a way to keep their own territory.
- **Wild dragons.** Some unbonded dragons keep mortal slaves, but they risk falling to the mortal kingdoms and their many dragons.
- **Dragon politics.** Dragons have politics of their own. They align with bonded kingdoms along their own internal factions, for their own reasons. The factions stay mysterious; the dragons don't bother translating them out of their own incomprehensible tongue.
- **Dragon axis: Old Dominion ↔ New Order.** Primal dragons want their rider to become more dragon; it's a little of a return to the old dominion of monsters. Evolved dragons want dragons to become more conscious and take part in the new order.
- **Cadet dragons.** Each cadet has a dragon that is bonded to her, or will bond to her later.
- **The protagonist's dragon.** Probably chosen from a diverse roster, though that's undecided. Either the user picks, or the dragon picks the user if it's left to the LLM.
- **Mated pairs.** Mated pairs link the dragons of other cadets with whichever dragon the protagonist bonds.
  - **[draft]** Every dragon the protagonist could bond is tied to at least one cadet's dragon, so the protagonist gets pulled into that cadet's orbit whichever dragon they end up with. A dragon may also speak to its mate's rider, so the protagonist will hear from a rival's dragon, and that rival will hear from theirs.
- Each dragon gets an IP voice anchor, the same way the cadets do.

### Dragon design variables
*The knobs every dragon gets set on. Dragons get ordinary character pages (`species: dragon`), with these as their fields. Broods are canon. The other variables are [draft].*

**1. Brood (titan line).** The titan a dragon descends from. It sets the body tendencies, the usual form of breath and what the dragon feels like to be near. Broods aren't the same thing as fae kin. A brood can nest anywhere and bond any kin.
- **Forge brood**: heavy and plated, with seams in the hide that glow when angry. Breath is a slag-heavy stream, or gas bombs. Smells of hot iron.
- **Storm brood**: long-winged and lean, with a crest that lifts with static. Breathes crackling near-plasma beams. Restless indoors.
- **Grave brood**: gaunt, with plates like exposed bone and tattered wings. Breathes rolling pyroclastic ash. Patient and quiet.
- **Thorn brood**: horn-crowned and barbed, with moss and lichen in the scale gaps. Breathes clinging wytchfire in sick greens and ambers. Territorial.
- **Gilt brood**: metallic scales that throw light, with a sun-disc frill. Breathes a white-gold stream that blinds before it burns. Vain.
- **Deep brood**: dark and slick, with big eyes made for the caverns. Breathes wytchfire in cold blues and violets. Rarely flies by day.

**2. Age and size [draft].** These set what a dragon can do and how it treats a cadet.
- **Whelp**: dog to pony sized. Can't bond yet. Kept deep in the caverns.
- **Young**: horse to wagon sized. Bondable, eager and reckless. Its bonds are the most volatile.
- **Prime**: house sized. Most bonds happen here. Strong opinions.
- **Elder**: hall sized. Bonds rarely, and when it does, it shakes the Aerie. These are the ones with a say in the agreement.
- **Ancient**: barely moves. May remember the titans. Bonds never, or only once in an age.

**3. Build [draft].** The silhouette at a glance.
- **Heavy**: armored, slow to turn, takes hits.
- **Racer**: lean, fast, fragile.
- **Serpentine**: long-bodied, turns tight, hard to ride.
- **Raptor**: compact and muscled, an ambusher.
- **Silhouette details:** horn shape and count, crests and frills, back spines, wing shape (broad soaring, narrow fast, tattered), and the tail end (club, spade, blade, whip or rattle).

**4. Hide [draft].** Scale type (plated, pebbled, overlapping, smooth), coloration, and natural features: glowing seams, metallic sheen, mineral growths, old scars.

**5. Breath [draft].** Its form (wytchfire, beam, ash, gas bombs or plain stream), its color, and how focused it is. Set by brood, with exceptions.

**6. Mind [draft].**
- **Lean**: where it sits on Old Dominion ↔ New Order.
- **Memory**: ancestral memory of the titan age, strong in the old and absent in the young.
- **What it wants from a rider**: the titan blood in her, or the fae herself.
- **How it sees fae**: as kin, as servants, or as the titans' bastards.

**7. Temperament and voice [draft].** An IP voice anchor, and how it treats its rider in public and in private.

**8. Standing in the caverns [draft].**
- **Rank**: elder, matriarch or patriarch, nobody, outcast.
- **Ties**: its mate, clutchmates, offspring, rivals, grudges.
- **History**: some dragons are old enough to have flown in the civil war. Some flew for the rebels. Some lost riders to the crown. Some were raised wild.
- **Faction**: which internal dragon faction it belongs to, if it will say.

**9. Sex and mating [draft].** Female or male, mated or unmated, clutches. The mated-pair rule runs through this.

**10. Bond pull [draft].** What draws a dragon to a cadet: the strength of her mark, a titan line that resonates, a temperament that matches, or something only the dragon knows.

**Roster card.** Name · Brood · Age · Sex · Build · Hide · Breath · Lean · Voice anchor · Wants from a rider · Ties · Bonded (or will bond) to.

## Lore entries
*Short, format-agnostic versions for a lorebook, scenario file or engine module.*
- **The Aedling Kingdom:** the large, diverse fae kingdom the Aerie serves. It's struggling with what that diversity entails. Its kin roughly represent regions, and other kin call them gilts (Solarkin), chalks (Lunarkin), cinders (Ashkin) and stumps (Woedkin).
- **The Aerie:** a dragon rider school at the peak of a semi-dormant volcano in the Aedling Kingdom, housed in a temple built by forgotten giants of unknown origin. Dragons perch on the peak and nest in the caverns inside the mountain. Entry is a climb up the mountain, and going back down is its own trial.
- **Marks:** skin markings from titan bloodlines, revealed in adulthood. They can take almost any design (script, runes, vines, patterns) and may be metallic or glowing. They show a fae can channel titanic magic. Only fae are marked. Crown law sends every marked fae to the Aerie, and hiding one is treason. A mark doesn't tie its bearer to a dragon.
- **Bloodlines:** mortal bloodlines are mixed, so which titan line manifests isn't always clear. High houses pride themselves on ties to specific lines, but nothing is guaranteed. Nobody knows whether a dragon's brood has to match its rider's line.
- **Bloodline mating:** houses prize marked females for bearing marked children. They would keep their daughters home to pair them off if the crown allowed it. Desirable cadets are courted as pairs, even lowborn ones if they're powerful enough. Neither the crown nor the order has any formal claim on a rider's children or pairing.
- **Titans:** primal, titanic creatures of a past age. Some fae priestesses bore their offspring, and both fae bloodlines and dragons descend from them.
- **Titan remnants:** degenerate descendants of the titans, diminutive and feral beside the old ones, but still dangerous.
- **Dragons:** mostly true western dragons, with four limbs and wings, descended from titans with no mortal blood. Their reasons are their own. Some lean toward the old dominion of monsters, and others toward the new order.
- **Dragon speech:** telepathic. A dragon can speak to anyone it chooses, but it usually speaks only to its rider or its mate's rider. Dragons talk freely among themselves, partly in a tongue of their own that nobody else can follow.
- **Dragon breath:** always some form of fire. It may be clinging wytchfire, crackling near-plasma beams, clouds of pyroclastic ash, or exploding balls of burning gas. Most can also breathe a plain stream of flame.
- **Why dragons bond:** since the primal age ended, mortals have raided every inch of land, breaking and stealing eggs. Bonded dragons are spared that. They keep a future and their own territory. Wild dragons that keep mortal slaves risk falling to kingdoms with many dragons.
- **Dragon politics:** dragons have internal factions of their own and align with bonded kingdoms for their own reasons. They don't explain their factions to anyone.
- **Bond:** the link between rider and dragon, possible because both descend from titans. A mark doesn't guarantee one, and cadets compete for dragons. Bonding is what lets a rider channel her mark and brings out her signature power.
- **The Kindling:** the agreed time when dragons choose their riders, down in the nesting caverns. A dragon can still bond whenever it likes. The saying goes: *Where does a ten-ton dragon sit? Wherever he wants.*
- **Unbonded cadets:** a marked cadet who survives without a bond can repeat the year or leave for mage school.
- **The Primaris Order:** a monastic order of fae that maintains the Aerie. Its members see themselves as keepers of the old titan cults where the bloodlines began. They'd return the world to the age of titans, but they defer to the dragons and tend whatever the dragons ask for.
- **The agreement:** the Aerie runs under a negotiated agreement between the Primaris Order, the crown and the dragons. Cadets are trained by crown military instructors.
- **Cadet death:** common. Tests have no guardrails, sparring goes all the way, and dragons are dragons.
- **Houses:** fae families with strong bloodlines that produce riders more often. They're a source of status, resentment and prejudice.
- **The civil war:** some Ashkin and Woedkin factions rebelled against the crown two generations ago.
- **Witch queens:** rulers of rival kingdoms who carry more titan blood than most.
